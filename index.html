Detecting AI agents that pose as your customers

Six AI agents, each hiding one more tell, against a Netomi Virtual Agent. What caught them, and where detection broke.

Netomi Architecture · November 2026

Why it matters

AI agents already contact support for customers. You need to know which chats are theirs.

Know who you're serving

An agent acting for a customer may need a structured channel, extra verification, or a person for sensitive requests.

Spot abuse at scale

Automated refund claims, account probing and data scraping all arrive as ordinary-looking chats.

Keep metrics honest

Containment and satisfaction figures mean little if some of the "customers" are agents.

How it's built

Detection runs on the existing channel. Nothing changes for the customer.

  • 01Netomi Virtual Agent. An order playbook answers the question and runs the VA's own checks.
  • 02Detection service. Scores browser, typing and mouse telemetry; the VA calls it through two MCP tools.
  • 03AI customer. A small local model, through the Chat API or driving the real chat widget.

Deception level 5 of 6 10 of 10 caught

Real keystrokes, slightly too fast and too even: caught by a thin margin.

input_stats caught all 10 at 133–148 ms between keys, against a human floor of 146 ms. About 10 ms slower per key would have passed.

The takeaway

No single signal holds. Stack imperfect ones.

  • 01Start cheap. Missing telemetry and automation flags catch the crudest agents at almost no cost.
  • 02Measure input against your own customers. Typing timing held to Level 5, with a thin margin.
  • 03Let the VA ask, probe and read style. Netomi playbooks carry those checks; they caught what the telemetry missed.
